            About the data

            Real Journey Time shows how long Dutch public transport really took on recorded days. It is a Dutch version of Tom Forth's Real Journey Time for buses in England.

            What "9 days in 10" means

            Leave at the time shown every day and you are on time on 9 days out of 10. If you travel there and back every weekday, that is one late arrival a week.

            Where the times come from

            Trains: every passenger train between Dutch stations for a year, from the Rijden de Treinen archive. Delays are in whole minutes.

            Metros, trams and buses: the recorded days, from Adriaan van Natijne's archive of the live feeds of Arriva, Connexxion, EBS, GVB, HTM, Keolis, Qbuzz and RET. Journeys after midnight are missing.

            How a journey is timed

            From the timetabled departure to the moment you arrive. You are at the stop a minute early. If the one you planned to catch is cancelled, or another gets there first, the first arrival counts, wait included. Disruptions with nothing running for 2 hours or more (such as strikes or closures) are left out, and so are journeys that were not recorded.

            How Plan chooses

            It tries one line, one change, or two changes when the middle ride is a train, on every recorded day. You walk at 4.8 km/h, up to 1 km to a stop or 2 km to a station. A change needs the walk between stops plus a minute, and 3 minutes at a station. If the timetable was different on a day and your departure did not run, that day counts as late.

            How the bike is timed

            The shortest legal cycle route on OpenStreetMap, without ferries, at the speed you choose. Traffic lights and parking are not counted. Trips over 80 km are not compared. 15 km/h is an everyday bike, 25 km/h an e-bike, and 45 km/h a speed pedelec, which may not use most cycle paths.

            How far

            You walk to a stop within 1 km, take one ride with no change, then walk. The ride takes as long as it did on 9 weekdays in 10 in that hour. People are counted where they live, from Statistics Netherlands' 100 m squares for 2024, to the nearest thousand.

            History

            The timetable on one weekday a year, the second Tuesday of October, from 2019 to 2025.

            Your privacy

            Plan runs in your browser. What you type in From and To goes to the Dutch government's address search, PDOK, to find the place. Your location is used only when you ask for it.
